How far the S&P 500 fell below a record close

Each line is a fall from a record, by trading day since it. A fall runs from a record close to the next record close, so mid-recovery dips sit inside the parent slide.

Vertical axis: percent below the record close that started the fall. The AI sell-off line extends with every market close the data refresh picks up.

The best-known falls

Depth is the lowest close before the next record; trading days run from the record close to that low; recovery days run from the record close to the first close back above it (the next record). Dashes: the ongoing slide has not recovered yet.

Data. Daily closes and intraday lows for the S&P 500 price index (^GSPC), the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC) and the PHLX Semiconductor Index (^SOX), pulled from the Yahoo Finance chart API by fetch_data.py and committed as data.js.

Slides. A slide runs from a record close (a close above every earlier close) to the next record close; its depth is the lowest close before that next record. Slides are counted from 1 Jan 1950, measured on the full history back to 1928. A slide counts as "reached 6%" once its intraday low traded 6% below the record close.

Indexes, not total returns. These are price indexes: dividends are excluded, so real losses were smaller and recoveries faster than drawn. "AI sell-off" is this site's name for the slide running from the record close. Nothing here is investment advice.

Refresh. python3 fetch_data.py re-downloads, re-detects the ongoing slide and rewrites data.js; --offline rebuilds from the cached fetch, --check verifies the known-crash table. The page concept follows kadoa.com's AI sell-off perspective, rebuilt on an independent pipeline.
